## G1-Design determination: REQUEST CHANGES — approval withheld

V2 resolves several important points, but **it is not yet sufficiently consistent or complete to authorize fixture implementation**. The remaining issues include mathematical contradictions, an incomplete factorial, undeclared dynamical state, and undefined implementation choices.

This is not a requirement that TAN succeed. A well-specified experiment expected to produce a negative result is acceptable; the protocol must first define exactly what will be implemented, measured, and interpreted.

I reviewed the complete [V2 protocol](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md). No files were changed, and no fixtures, models, or simulations were executed.

### Accepted revisions

I accept the following at the specification level:

- The disclosure and non-conforming designation of the preliminary scaffold.
- Quadratic metric alignment for the P1-A reference.
- Replacement of the mixed current/voltage expression with normalized equations.
- Post-cue separation as a design requirement.
- Separation of H1, H2, and H3, and of transport from internal composition.
- The two-stage authorization boundary.
- TOST-based equivalence and confidence-bound-based fidelity decisions **in principle**, subject to the corrections below.

These accepted items do not need another conceptual rewrite.

## Remaining sign-off blockers

### 1. The baseline-validity condition is impossible for \(N=2\)

The [validity condition](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:147) requires

\[
\operatorname{Acc}>\operatorname{Chance}+0.50.
\]

Using the protocol’s ordered-pair chance definition,

\[
\operatorname{Chance}(N=2)=\frac{1}{2(2-1)}=0.5,
\]

so the requirement becomes **\(\operatorname{Acc}>1\)**. No model can satisfy it.

Required corrections:

- Define `Acc` precisely: ordered address correctness, operand delivery, or joint success are different endpoints.
- Replace this condition with a feasible, registered performance floor or an M0-relative noninferiority criterion.
- Specify operand-error tolerances, abstention/deadline handling, and whether equivalence must hold separately in every primary condition.
- Specify the actual paired-proportion interval method. Merely listing “Wilson intervals” does not define an interval for the paired difference; that requires accounting for the joint outcomes. ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9839354/))
- Freeze fixture sample sizes/seeds and the statistical methods’ boundary-case behavior.

The \(\pm0.005\) margin and conventional 90% interval containment are acceptable as a TOST framework, but they do not supply the missing endpoint or sampling design. ([europepmc.org](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28736600))

Also align Stop Rule 2’s confidence conventions: **one-sided 95% bounds** and the endpoints of a **central 95% interval** are not interchangeable. Success, failure, and inconclusive must use the same registered bounds.

### 2. The stated two-variable state is not dynamically closed

The [state inventory](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:33) says only \([u,A]\) exists, but the firing rule uses `t_last_spike` and a refractory hold.

Two units can have identical \(u,A\) while differing in remaining refractory time. The same subsequent input can then produce different responses. Therefore, **\([u,A]\) alone does not determine future evolution** under the registered rules.

Required correction: include a refractory state, for example

\[
\mathbf z(t)=[u(t),A(t),r(t)]^\top,
\]

where \(r\) is remaining refractory time. Specify its initialization, evolution, and treatment in every intervention. A “full-state reset” must address this state too.

The following implementation-defining quantities are also still unspecified:

- \(w_K,w_V,\mu_{\mathrm{bg}},A_{\max}\), and \(\phi(q)\);
- initial \(A\);
- novelty outside event presentation;
- whether \(\mu_{\mathrm{bg}}\) is fixed or updated online;
- how the stated \(A_{\max}\) bound is enforced or guaranteed;
- whether \(A\) continues evolving during refractory periods;
- the analog decoder and its calibration procedure.

These must be fixed numerically or through an explicit, independently calibrated selection procedure before implementation. If the background estimate evolves online, its state must also be declared.

### 3. The proposed comparison family is not the stated \(2\times2\) factorial

In the [model table](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:103), the two proposed factors are:

- membrane retained versus post-event reset;
- gate dynamic versus constant \(A_0\).

Their four cells are:

| Membrane condition | Dynamic gate | Constant \(A_0\) |
|---|---|---|
| Retained | M1 | M2b |
| Post-event reset | M2a | **Missing** |

M2c does not fill the missing cell: resetting \(A\) to zero after events is not the same intervention as maintaining \(A(t)\equiv A_0\).

**Required correction:** add the missing cell and retain full-state reset as a separate control, or stop describing the present family as a full factorial.

Two additional corrections are necessary:

- **M3 duplicates M1’s declared access condition.** Both have no candidate-buffer access. M3 can instead be an interface-invariance/leakage fixture, but it is not an additional history-removal contrast unless a distinct buffer-enabled parent is specified.
- **Mean-gate matching is not automatically drive matching.** In general,
  \[
  \int A(t)I_{\mathrm{content}}(t)\,dt
  \ne
  \bar A\int I_{\mathrm{content}}(t)\,dt.
  \]
  Here, gate and current both depend on the keys. Either call the control “mean-gate matched” and limit the attribution, or specify an independent calibration criterion for the claimed drive match, including residual mismatch reporting.

### 4. The 10 ms deadline still conflicts with the presentation schedule

The [timeline](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:70) specifies:

1. Query presentation lasting 10 ms.
2. A 5 or 10 ms readout window **following** that presentation.

Consequently, readout completes **15 or 20 ms after cue onset**, not within 10 ms.

Choose one explicit convention:

- Record and decode within \([T_{\mathrm{cue}},T_{\mathrm{cue}}+10\text{ ms})\) for a genuine 10 ms cue-to-delivery deadline; or
- Retain the subsequent readout window and report the longer end-to-end latency.

The protocol must also reconcile **10 ms per operand** in §6 with **composition under 10 ms** in §7. Parallel channels, sequential queries, and external operand buffering have different latency and resource costs.

Finally, the \(W=5\) contract has disappeared. The [frozen generator](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/task_generator.py:76) places candidates into five slots; V2 schedules only \(N\) event slots. That is a task-adapter change, not automatically an identical temporal input.

Specify the mapping, target ages, eligibility convention, and whether eviction testing is retained or explicitly deferred. Note that \(T_{\mathrm{enc}}\) includes the final 10 ms ISI: zero post-encoding delay is therefore not zero delay from the final event’s offset.

### 5. The spike budget and decoder codebooks need correction

The [spike contract](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:122) contains an arithmetic error:

\[
\left\lfloor10/2\right\rfloor+1=6,
\]

not five.

Five spikes can be a consistent budget for a half-open 10 ms window with a minimum 2 ms interspike interval. Freeze the interval endpoints, initial refractory eligibility, and whether the rule is \(>\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}\) or \(\ge\tau_{\mathrm{ref}}\).

The codebooks also need exact definitions:

- Six equally spaced reconstruction values **including** −3 and 3 have spacing \(6/5=1.2\).
- Six equal-width bins of width 1 are another valid choice, with midpoint reconstructions \(-2.5,-1.5,\ldots,2.5\).

Specify which is intended.

For the latency decoder, explicitly enforce the 0.5 ms quantization. A 100-element array sampled at 0.1 ms exposes finer timing than the claimed 20-bin code unless the decoder is restricted accordingly. Define no-spike handling and prohibit use of later spikes for a first-spike-only decoder.

There is also an important **analytical benchmark to register before simulation**. For independent \(V_A,V_B\sim U[-3,3]\), independently encoded by equal-bin midpoint quantizers with \(M\) bins, the quantization errors are independent uniforms of width \(6/M\), giving

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[|e_A\pm e_B|\right]=\frac{2}{M}.
\]

Thus this ideal codec benchmark gives:

- \(M=6:\ E_{\mathrm{comp}}=1/3\);
- \(M=20:\ E_{\mathrm{comp}}=0.10\).

These are calculations for that specified codec—not simulations and not universal bounds on all spike codes or joint-composition circuits. Both coarse codes may legitimately remain registered boundary controls, but their expected failure must not be interpreted as evidence against membrane computation.

### 6. H3 is separated conceptually but still lacks a circuit specification

[Pathway B](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:95) introduces a “two-channel / two-neuron circuit,” while the specified dynamics describe one unit with a unary query current \(\phi(q)\).

Missing are:

- the exact number of units and total state budget;
- assignment of \(q_A,q_B\) to inputs;
- inter-unit coupling equations;
- how `Op` changes neural evolution;
- the output state and fixed decoder;
- treatment of any first-operand storage.

There is no operation-dependent term in the supplied unit equations. Therefore, H3 is currently a hypothesis without a fully specified implementation.

Two acceptable amendments are available:

1. Specify the complete circuit and decoder; or
2. Mark H3 **deferred / not tested**, and seek approval for a narrower H1/H2 carrier audit.

For H2, also clarify that the quadratic metric belongs to M0 unless a dynamic implementation is actually supplied. Merely locking the reference score does not cause the additive query-current model to implement that metric.

### 7. Several G1 rows remain objectives rather than executable acceptance contracts

The [gate table](C:/Users/李则徐/Downloads/TAN_Review/P0_20260917_181757/p1_experiments/p1b/P1B_PRE_REGISTRATION_PROTOCOL_V2.md:158) needs the following corrections:

- **G1.3:** A sampled query-blind control need not equal theoretical chance exactly. Specify a finite-sample acceptance/alert rule. State which 100% trap assertions apply to the oracle and harness, versus which are scientific outcomes for TAN.
- **G1.5:** Match the **complete declared state**, not only membrane voltage. Specify the collision construction’s valid domain, nonzero denominator, subthreshold/no-reset assumptions, and numerical tolerance.
- **G1.6:** Do not make a desired scientific failure the general conformance criterion. Any asserted impossibility must name the code, decoder, distribution, loss, and assumptions.
- **G1.7:** Specify the integration method, comparison norm, physical comparison times, and separate continuous-state versus spike-event tolerances. A blanket \(10^{-6}\) “drift” requirement across threshold/reset discontinuities is not an adequate numerical contract.
- **G1.7 additionally:** A 2 ms refractory period is 20 steps at 0.1 ms but 40 steps at 0.05 ms. Refinement must preserve physical timing and decoder resolution.
- **G1.8–G1.9:** Freeze fixture cases/counts/seeds, uncertainty procedures, and the required trace/output schema—not just the names of methods and the existence of SHA-256 logging.

A valid TAN failure must remain reportable. It must not force protocol changes simply because an oracle-success assertion was incorrectly applied to the candidate model.

## Authorization disposition

Answers to the four sign-off questions are:

1. **Initial scaffold violations:** disclosure is accepted; replacement-design closure is incomplete.
2. **Factorial localization:** not yet valid as the stated \(2\times2\) design.
3. **TOST and 10 ms contract:** acceptable principles, but the present threshold, timing, and decoder specifications are not approvable.
4. **Fixture implementation:** **not authorized by this review**. Full simulation remains blocked.

The next deliverable should be a focused V2.1 amendment or specification annex resolving these items. It need not add more models or pursue a positive result; explicitly narrowing the audit and registering expected negative boundaries is acceptable.
