## Scope that must accompany results

1. **A fixed possible onset, with no event on catches.** [Protocol line13](PREREGISTRATION.md:13) should be read as fixing the observation clock at15. Given `S=1`, the actual onset is15 and `H(onset|S=1)=0`; given `S=0`, there is no event. If an event-time variable is defined on all trials with alphabet `{no event,15}`, its entropy is `H(S)=1 bit` under the balanced prior. It merely recodes presence/absence, not variation in onset timing. Recommended wording: “The possible event time is fixed at15; catch trials contain no event. The study measures presence at that known time, not unknown-event localization.” This also qualifies the shorter “onset entropy0” shorthand in earlier proposal/review notes.

2. **The prior belongs to the v9 diagnostic extension.** [Protocol line11](PREREGISTRATION.md:11) correctly makes the balanced prior explicit. The original runner always presents the step ([statistical_validation.py:174](../../Temporal-Attention-Neuron/snapshot/code/experiments/statistical_validation.py#L174)); catches were added in v9. Accordingly, `I(S;O)` is for the imposed step/catch benchmark, not an original published class-information score, estimated natural event prevalence, or capacity optimized over input priors. The counterfactual pair is used only for inference, never as the single-trial decoder input.

3. **Conditional Gaussian-path bits do not label spikes by cause.** [Protocol lines29–31](PREREGISTRATION.md:29) order the information allocation as stimulus first, path dependence conditional on stimulus, then deletion randomness conditional on both. For deterministic native outputs, `I(N;O|S)=H(O|S)` uses the full relevant path and fixed model/initial state. It includes past-noise effects and signal–noise interactions. It is neither a unique assignment of “noise-origin spikes” nor new information created by a neuron. A positive `Delta_path` means count detail about N is discarded under this conditioning; it does not say every discarded event or detail is harmful. No fraction of continuous input noise removed is measured.

4. **C→D is an external representation change.** [Protocol line33](PREREGISTRATION.md:33) correctly distinguishes the count from the fixed binary readout. Data processing here concerns an external five-step counter followed by an at-least-one rule, both granted the observation clock. It does not identify the native voltage threshold, attention, or gate as the causal noise filter. A positive nuisance-detail lower bound with a small signal-loss upper bound would establish useful compression for this declared binary label. It would not establish a new neuronal mechanism, recovery of within-window timing, or recovery of arbitrary waveforms.

5. **Analytic thinning is the same stochastic control, evaluated with less Monte Carlo noise.** [Protocol line21](PREREGISTRATION.md:21) preserves v9's label-blind deletion channel and probability. The kernel average targets its mask-unobserved output distribution; it does not make the physical channel deterministic or improve its behavior. The saved per-history probability vector is analytical bookkeeping, not an extra analog output delivered to the decoder. Native counts used to integrate the kernel remain unavailable to that decoder. Native and thinned quantities share the same stimulus prior, Gaussian history ensemble, output alphabet, and observation window. Marginalizing U changes the evaluation method; it does not grant equal physical resources, matched fresh activity, or superior hardware efficiency.

   An earlier `THEORY_PROPOSAL.md` sentence called analytic integration a “different estimand” from v9's realized masks. The selected protocol is more precise: it is the **same underlying randomized channel/population functional**, with a marginalized empirical-kernel evaluation rather than one realized-mask empirical evaluation. Both differ as finite-sample records and must not be numerically equated across different batches. No conditioning on a revealed U mask is introduced. Also qualify “analytic results are expectations”: probabilities and event counts are averaged over deletion; entropy/MI are then evaluated on the appropriate marginalized pmfs. They are **not** averages of mask-conditioned MI, nor generally the expectation of a finite-sample plug-in MI estimate across realized thinning masks. Entropy is nonlinear in its pmf. The separately defined mean conditional kernel entropy U_O remains the valid conditional-entropy term in the chain rule.

6. **The two uncertainty statements have different coverage.** [Protocol line37](PREREGISTRATION.md:37) gives pointwise descriptive bootstrap intervals. [Lines39–53](PREREGISTRATION.md:39) separately give a simultaneous population certificate for the two native models. The union bounds need iid history rows within each model/class, not independent paired classes/models. The two error-entropy bounds protect both stimulus-information loss and conditional-path-detail claims; zero empirical errors do not imply zero population error. The certificate does not extend automatically to integrated fractional thinning outcomes. Any zero lower bound must remain an inconclusive bound, not be reinterpreted as proof that no nuisance detail exists. “At least95% confidence” describes the procedure's repeated-sampling coverage under the declared model; it is not a posterior probability that this particular model explanation is true.

7. **Output information and activity are separate from energy.** [Protocol line57](PREREGISTRATION.md:57) keeps the original late-rate field and resource proxies separate. Thinned LIF retains native LIF computation and generated spikes. Neither the analytic marginalization nor lower observed entropy establishes equal gain, equal biological state/energy resources, ATP/joules, or bits-per-energy improvement. If both native models support the same external compression result, report that shared outcome without selecting an architecture winner.

## Primary sources support mathematics, not neuronal conclusions

- The finite-output/general-conditioning identity and its chain-rule applications are standard. [Gray, §5.5, Lemmas5.5.6–5.5.7](https://ee.stanford.edu/~gray/it.pdf#page=143) explicitly permits a finite output with a general conditioning variable. This justifies the mixed Gaussian/discrete treatment, not a biological interpretation or a causal partition of events.
- [Hoeffding's original bounded-sum result](https://www.csee.umbc.edu/~lomonaco/f08/643/hwk643/Hoeffding.pdf) supplies each fixed-subset concentration inequality; the proof's finite union over four histograms and62 subsets is the stated application. The original PDF was retrieved; the existing proof supplies the explicit specialization and coverage argument.
- [Audenaert's original theorem1 and classical reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0610146v1) support the six-category entropy-continuity modulus. The paper explicitly reduces to probability vectors and Shannon entropy. Using that classical result does not introduce a quantum-neuron claim. The monotone-radius step and fixed alphabet remain necessary.
- The exact one-sided binomial bounds are justified by the self-contained tail inversion in [the selected proof](POPULATION_CERTIFICATE_PROOF.md:33). The proof correctly discloses that the original Clopper–Pearson publisher text was unavailable, rather than claiming to have inspected it. No unsupported quotation or stronger coverage property is required.
- [Paninski's original estimator study](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/lcv/paninski-infoEst-2003.pdf) supports caution about finite-sample bias and resampling uncertainty. It does not establish the empirical results, validate the original repository estimator, or supply the separate v10 coverage guarantee; that guarantee comes from the frozen certificate's proof.
