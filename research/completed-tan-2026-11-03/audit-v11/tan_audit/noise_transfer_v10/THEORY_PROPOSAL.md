## Exact identities and their limits

Use base2 logs, `0 log2(0)=0`. For a deterministic native output `Y=f(S,N)` such as K or D:

`I(S;Y) = H(Y) - H(Y|S)`;

`I(N;Y|S) = H(Y|S)`;

`H(Y) = I(S;Y) + I(N;Y|S)`.

These are exact population identities, not new theory. They use finite output entropy and do not involve differential-entropy subtraction for continuous noise. The finite-alphabet conditional-entropy framework follows [Shannon's original communication theory](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf).

The label for the second term should be **conditional dependence on the Gaussian path**, not automatically harmful noise or lost signal. Threshold interactions between signal and noise are included. The decomposition assigns signal first, then nuisance conditional on signal; reversing the chain-rule order generally changes the allocation. It is not a unique causal partition into signal-origin and noise-origin spikes. Nor does it estimate a fraction of the continuous input's total noise information removed: no such normalized denominator is supplied.

For the binary detector, write `t=P(D=1|S=1)` and `f=P(D=1|S=0)`. Then

`I(S;D)=h2((t+f)/2) - (h2(t)+h2(f))/2`.

This formula shows directly why a new binary MI score alone repeats v9. Since D is a deterministic function of K, `I(S;D)<=I(S;K)` and `I(N;D|S)<=I(N;K|S)`. A reduction in conditional count variability after thresholding can preserve the chosen binary task while discarding graded responses. That is ordinary task-dependent compression, not newly created information or proof of a native biological computation.

## Integrate thinning rather than confounding two noise sources

Keep `p=529/1013`. Native LIF produces K; independent output-deletion uniforms U produce thinned count Y. Conditional on S and N, and hence on native spike positions and K:

`B[k,y]=P(Y=y|K=k)=choose(k,y)*p^y*(1-p)^(k-y)`, for `0<=y<=k`.

The marginal distribution for condition s is exactly `P(Y=y|s)=sum_k P(K=k|s)*B[k,y]`. Thus

`H_U = H(Y|S,N) = (1/2)*sum_s sum_k P(K=k|s)*H(B[k,:])`;

`I_N = I(N;Y|S) = H(Y|S)-H_U`;

`H(Y) = I(S;Y) + I_N + H_U`.

Here `H_U=I(U;Y|S,N)` because the complete map is deterministic given S,N,U. This is the retained output variability attributable to independent thinning *conditional on the inputs*, not the full entropy of the uniform random generator. Given K the output-count distribution is independent of the remaining Gaussian-path details, so `I(N;Y|S)=I(K;Y|S)` as well. Shared thinning uniforms between the two counterfactual conditions in v9 do not alter these single-trial marginal identities. Analytic integration needs no additional U draws and is a different estimand from v9's one realized thinning mask; preserve and label both.

For the thinned binary detector, conditional positive probability is `r(k)=1-(1-p)^k`; replace `H(B[k,:])` by `h2(r(k))` in the same identities. Native models use identity channels and `H_U=0`. ZERO_OUTPUT has every entropy and information term zero. A synthetic label oracle `Y=S` can validate arithmetic at1bit signal information and zero conditional nuisance, but must never be treated as a neuron or granted to a decoder.

Required arithmetic checks, before scientific interpretation: every channel row sums to1; every conditional output pmf sums to1; all terms nonnegative within declared numerical tolerance; chain-rule residual near machine precision; both data-processing inequalities hold; p=1 recovers native LIF count distribution and p=0 recovers silence. These two endpoint checks validate the analytic helper only; they are not added empirical model arms or a probability sweep.

## Finite samples, uncertainty, and nulls

For an empirical batch, frequencies of K conditional on S determine a six-bin distribution. All the identities and analytic thinning calculations are exact for that **empirical input distribution**. They are estimates of population functionals, not exact population MI. Retain all six possible bins, including zeros; no jitter, adaptive binning, KSG, arbitrary pseudocounts, Miller–Madow patch, normalized “efficiency” ratio, or outcome-selected decoder. Finite-sample entropy/MI bias and misleading uncertainty are established concerns, not defects unique to TAN; [Paninski's primary methodological study](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/lcv/paninski-infoEst-2003.pdf) is relevant here.

Resample whole independent Gaussian-history groups, preserving each group's step/catch native counts and both models, then reapply the exact thinning kernel inside each bootstrap replicate. Do not bootstrap individual time bins, or treat analytic output probabilities as independent samples. Any percentile intervals are descriptive empirical intervals; sparse or unseen count categories can remain absent in every resample. A fresh batch does not remove this finite-sample limitation.

Zero empirical detector errors need a nondegenerate population safeguard. For n independent histories in each class, if both native detector miss and false-alarm counts are zero, inversion of `P(0 errors|q)=(1-q)^n` with a union bound gives simultaneous confidence at least `1-alpha` that both class error probabilities are at most `e=1-(alpha/2)^(1/n)`. The pairing between classes does not invalidate that union bound. For the balanced prior, population detector error is then at most e. With `E=S xor D`, `H(S|D)=H(E|D)<=H(E)<=h2(e)` when e<=1/2, yielding

`I(S;K) >= I(S;D) >= 1-h2(e)`.

This is a conservative population lower bound, not a claim of1bit or zero population error. For nonzero observed errors, use predeclared exact binomial upper limits per class and the same entropy argument; allocate alpha across every model for which a simultaneous claim is intended. Do not apply the zero-success binomial formula directly to analytically integrated thinned probabilities, which are bounded averages rather than observed Bernoulli trials. Those require their own justified bounded-mean interval or remain explicitly descriptive.

Minimal null controls are exact silence and the analytic channel endpoint checks. A label permutation could be an additional estimator diagnostic, but its paired exchangeability assumptions and randomization must be specified; it is unnecessary for this narrow closure and should not be added opportunistically.
