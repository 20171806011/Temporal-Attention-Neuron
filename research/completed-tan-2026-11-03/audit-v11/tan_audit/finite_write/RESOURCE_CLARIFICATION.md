# Resource interpretation after the fixed run

This note clarifies accounting; it does not change the frozen algorithm, inputs, conditions or results.

The simulated writer has three binary64 payload locations (K,V,S) and three allocated binary64 scratch locations. Its explicit stage state has four values and can be encoded in two bits. Scratch is cleared between updates, but its allocated storage still counts: 192 payload bits plus 192 scratch bits, before controller, current input and output resources. Python/Numpy object overhead and evaluator ledgers are not a physical circuit estimate.

The two stage bits assume externally supplied event-active, onset/offset, reset and read-clock signals. They are not a complete physical controller. A100 needs a 10 kHz update schedule; FAST10 uses the same first-capture schedule and a 615.3 kHz second-write schedule aligned to a 10 ms event. The simulation's loop counters implement this externally assumed schedule; they are not hidden model memories accessible at query.

If a standalone implementation generates exact per-event step termination with an ordinary binary counter, a shared counter able to represent 0..100 needs 7 additional bits for A100, and one able to represent 0..6153 needs 13 for FAST10. Reusing that counter between capture/write yields a concrete digital-control allowance of 9 or 15 bits including the two stage bits. This is a sufficient conventional counter allocation, not a proven minimum. It still excludes oscillator state, timing precision, reset circuitry, clock distribution and external stimulus scheduling. These counters were not simulated as payload or used by the decoder. No claim of an autonomous two-bit physical controller is warranted.

The largest declared FAST10 feedback pole is 615.271 per ms, a relaxation time of about 1.6253 microseconds. A numerical update count and a physical feedback bandwidth are different resources. The experiment verifies the literal discrete recurrence and compares a continuous reference; it does not build a multiplier/divider circuit or measure its bandwidth. The current k,v signals must remain available throughout each event. Input and query/output ports use binary64 precision in the simulator.

Per second-write update, the explicit sequential scratch schedule performs three subtractions, three multiplications (including multiplication by the fixed h*gamma constant), and one addition. It performs no runtime data-dependent division. Capture performs four multiplications, two subtractions and two additions per step across K,V. Shared G, gamma and h are computed offline from declared duration/domain constants; this calibration is not a free adaptive inversion. Query performs one subtraction, one multiplication and one addition per requested key, and exports its result before clearing scratch. Algebraic query latency and converter energy are not modeled.

The primary field `scratch_active_real_ms=60` means provisioned scratch capacity during the two 10 ms active windows: three locations times 20 ms. It is not an integral of instantaneous nonzero scratch values, measured switching time, or energy. `scratch_allocated_real_ms=300` reserves those locations for the entire 100 ms episode. Payload is likewise allocated throughout the episode. No analog storage cost is inferred from the count alone.

Coordinate-square, squared-update-rate and total-variation integrals retain separate K, V and S units. They are descriptive dynamical proxies, not quantities that can be summed into ATP, joules, electrical power or equal-noise costs. Hold switches set payload derivatives to zero between designated phases; their retention noise, leakage, refresh and energy are not modeled. The gain calibration also depends on reset, time constant and exact capture duration.

The calibration constant is update-rule-specific. G_Euler=1.5773675301 compensates exactly 100 declared Euler steps. A continuous tau10 capture over10ms instead needs G_cont=1.5819767069. Holding G_Euler unchanged in continuous capture leaves relative undercharge0.0029135554 and an analytic converged first-query closure error as large as1.01288. The saved continuous-reference outputs integrate only the second slope stage from the actual Euler-captured anchor; they do not validate the whole mechanism as a continuous circuit. This is a disclosed analytic limitation, not a new tuned arm.

The appropriate conclusion is conditional implementability of this enriched numerical mechanism at declared precision, timing and gain, plus a resource obstruction for its fixed 100-step constant-gain version. It is not an implementation in the registered TAN state structure, nor a total hardware-resource comparison.
