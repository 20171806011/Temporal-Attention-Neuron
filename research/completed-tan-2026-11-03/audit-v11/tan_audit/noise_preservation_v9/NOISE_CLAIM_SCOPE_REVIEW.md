## Preservation/null coverage and recommended wording

Within the four reviewed files, there is no explicit always-zero-output control, joint false-alarm/detection criterion, event recall/precision result, or noise-versus-signal information comparison establishing Task B signal preservation. The qualitative true-transition assertion above is the closest positive claim. The complete [MASTER_CLAIM_LEDGER.csv](../../Temporal-Attention-Neuron/MASTER_CLAIM_LEDGER.csv#L1) has 11 claims (rows 2–12) on state sufficiency, geometry, retrieval, routing, binding, and composition; none is a Stage 1 Task B preservation/null claim. CLM-03's qualified retrieval result at [4](../../Temporal-Attention-Neuron/MASTER_CLAIM_LEDGER.csv#L4) and CLM-11's noisy addressability result at [12](../../Temporal-Attention-Neuron/MASTER_CLAIM_LEDGER.csv#L12) concern different tasks and cannot fill this gap.

Logical limitation, not a newly measured result: an output that is always zero can have no false spikes and carries no noise information, while also discarding the desired response. A score rewarding only reduced false spikes or reduced noise MI therefore cannot by itself distinguish selective filtering from indiscriminate suppression.

Suggested bounded wording: “Under the recorded Task B settings, TAN has a lower reported false-spike rate than LIF. This establishes reduced spiking in the scored interval; signal preservation and the tradeoff between false alarms and missed events are not established by that metric alone.” Use “scored interval” only after the primary audit confirms the exact scorer. Preserve the calibration and unperformed paired-analysis caveats alongside any quoted numerical comparison.
