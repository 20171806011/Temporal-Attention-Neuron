# Pre-execution Task B signal-preservation design review

Status: design review only, 2026-11-02. No new native-model runs, generated diagnostic outcomes, or threshold fitting were performed for this review. This is advice for a separately frozen protocol, not a retrospective registration. An independent statistical-design reviewer agreed on the primary window, fixed detector, and pairing requirements.

## What the original experiment measures

The frozen Task B uses 60 samples, a zero baseline through index 14, amplitude 1.5 from index 15, Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.4, and lower clipping at zero. It measures the mean spike count on indices `[20:60)` and calls that quantity `false_spike_rate`; it supplies no desired spike train or event-by-event false-positive labels. Native parameters are LIF leak 0.8/threshold 2 and TAN window 10/leak 0.8/threshold 1, with all initial states zero. TAN has `use_asym_rect=False`: the attention query retains signed surprise, while the gate is `tanh(abs(S))`. It is not the asymmetric novelty-gate model used elsewhere.

Sources: `snapshot/code/experiments/statistical_validation.py:79–132,174–201`; `snapshot/manuscript/sections/experiments.tex:113–119,139–156`. The published means 0.5065 and 0.2645, original labels, and historical PASS verdict should remain intact. Their interpretation needs a supplementary diagnostic.

Two structural cautions follow without simulation. First, a silent output achieves the best possible original score. Second, a clean 1.5 plateau drives the native LIF above threshold every two steps after onset: 1.5 then 0.8(1.5)+1.5=2.7, followed by reset. Sustained output therefore need not be caused by noise. TAN's window is not entirely filled with plateau samples until index 24; the original `[20:60)` window includes four samples of this adaptation period. Do not silently rename or move the historical window.

## Recommended minimal diagnostic

The measurable benefit to examine is **reduced late output with preserved evidence of the original upward step**. This does not test amplitude reconstruction, unknown-onset detection, general information preservation, or physical energy efficiency.

1. Reproduce the original 50 seeds and metric exactly, before reporting supplementary diagnostics. Record source/data hashes and native traces. Original replay and new diagnostic samples must be clearly separated.
2. Freeze one fresh modest batch, for example 256 independent Gaussian paths generated by a dedicated recorded seed. For each path, compare the original step with an all-zero no-step baseline using the same Gaussian draws and the same clipping. Both native models receive every resulting waveform, each from reset. Store/hash inputs before model evaluation. Pairing is in pre-clipping Gaussian noise; the clipped step and no-step signals do not differ by exactly 1.5 at every sample.
3. Add deterministic noise-free step and no-step references, once each per model. Repeating these references across random seeds does not create independent observations.
4. Keep `[20:60)` as the original late-rate endpoint. Fix `[15:20)` as the primary signal window because these are exactly the five onset samples excluded by the historical endpoint. The simplest task-relevant readout is the prespecified rule **step present iff this window contains at least one spike**. Report hit rate, false-alarm rate, specificity, and balanced accuracy. Its threshold is the same fixed count of one for both models; no training or calibration occurs. It is explicitly a new known-onset presence diagnostic.
5. Include an all-zero output surrogate. It has original late rate zero and balanced accuracy 0.5 on balanced step/no-step conditions. This exposes the original endpoint's inability to distinguish selective signaling from gain collapse. It does not claim that a real silent neuron consumes zero energy.

The five-step detector is a narrow recovery test, not an optimal decoder. Failure of this fixed detector would not prove absence of signal in other timings or counts. A ten-step onset window `[15:25)` can be justified by TAN's window length only if frozen as a secondary endpoint before outcomes; it must not replace the primary window after observing results. For a minimal study it is reasonable to omit it.

## Matching output activity without retuning neurons

Do not adjust input amplitudes, native thresholds, or leak to match a preferred outcome. An optional, small control for indiscriminate output suppression is **LIF output thinning** with constant keep probability

`p = 0.2645 / 0.5065 = 529 / 1013`.

This ratio comes from the frozen published Task B means in `snapshot/data/experiment_results/stats_validation_summary.csv`, not fresh diagnostic data. Freeze it and the independent thinning random stream before running. Apply it to every native LIF output time without access to labels, TAN trial outputs, or onset/late windows. Shared thinning uniforms within each paired step/no-step realization are acceptable if declared. It approximately matches the historical expected late spike budget; report actual new-sample mismatch rather than forcing equality. Underlying LIF states and reset events remain the native ones, so this is an output surrogate, not a distinct physical neuron or an energy-matched mechanism.

This optional control can ask whether TAN's joint late-output and onset-detection behavior exceeds a simple output attenuation explanation under that budget convention. It cannot establish intrinsic attention superiority. Do not thin LIF to each trial's TAN count: that imports another model's signal and quietly changes the information contract.

A temporal shuffle is an alternative timing control, not a necessary additional arm. It preserves the exact full-trial spike count but can retain class information through that count, so it is not a guaranteed chance-level null. Avoid adding both controls merely to obtain favorable comparisons.

## Statistical and resource accounting

Use the entire independent noise-realization group as the sampling unit: both stimulus conditions, both native models, and any surrogate travel together through a paired bootstrap. Freeze its seed and replicate count. Do not resample individual time bins as independent data. Include paired differences in late rate and balanced accuracy; preserve all unfavorable comparisons. Clean references are deterministic checks rather than statistical replicates.

An optional count comparison with the same-noise counterfactual gives a tie-adjusted paired win probability, `P(N_step>N_no-step)+0.5 P(equal)`. This is not ordinary cross-history AUC or a deployable single-trial accuracy. The fixed detector already supplies an interpretable single-trial statistic, so no ranking endpoint is required. If any AUC is added, its definition and paired resampling must be explicit.

Report full-trial/onset/late spike counts and, if independently instrumented, sums of absolute effective current, squared effective current, and squared pre-reset membrane candidate, with their exact sampling conventions. These are dimensionless resource proxies, not ATP. TAN has a ten-sample retained history plus membrane state and attention arithmetic; LIF has a membrane state. Native thresholds and internal current transformations differ. Equal external waveforms, equal numerical integrals, or equal output counts do not establish equal physical energy. Thinning alters observed outputs while retaining the simulated internal computation.

## Frozen interpretation rules

- Lower late output alone supports lower late output, even when its original statistical contrast is extremely strong.
- Lower late output together with a clean onset response and above-null noisy step detection supports selective event signaling on this specific fixed-step task. It does not demonstrate general denoising or sustained amplitude recovery.
- Strong detection in both native models with different late rates is compatible with different coding strategies; sustained LIF coding is not automatically noise corruption.
- Weak detection or silence narrows the interpretation to output suppression under the tested readout. It does not establish a universal information-loss theorem.
- A TAN advantage over fixed-ratio output-thinned LIF would reject that particular indiscriminate suppression explanation, under the explicit output-budget convention. It would not establish equal-energy superiority or isolate the causal contribution of attention versus the gate/history/threshold.

Stop after original replay, clean checks, the single fixed paired table, independent trajectory/metric validation, and complete ledger. Do not broaden to threshold/noise sweeps, fitted decoders, or the separate mutual-information/cascade audit. Preserve the independently supported P1A evidence and every historical Task B label.
